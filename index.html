Forecourt & Test Drive Checks

1. Underseal Tap Test (Key / Screwdriver) Deal Breaker

THE KEY CHECK ON THIS TRUCK. Fresh underseal applied just before sale. The MOT tester couldn't see through it.

Take a torch. Tap the chassis rails behind the rear wheels, the rear leaf spring hangers and the crossmembers firmly. Look for bubbles or lumps under the underseal, flakes or rust dust on the ground, and underseal sprayed over brake pipes or the exhaust (a rushed job).

Solid "Clink" / "Thud": Structure is sound. The underseal is protection.

Soft "Crunch" / Hollow: Rotten behind the underseal. WALK AWAY.

2. Magnet Filler Test Deal Breaker

Press a magnet on any thick, lumpy or uneven patches of underseal along the frame, especially near the rear springs.

Falls Off / Weak Stick: Hole packed with filler and undersealed over. WALK AWAY.

3. Cold Start & Timing Chain Deal Breaker

Feel the bonnet first. Start it from cold yourself.

Listen: Metallic rattle for the first few seconds points to a stretched chain or tensioner.
Look: Heavy smoke, or engine / glow plug / DPF lights staying on. Low-mileage short trips can clog the DPF.

Verdict: Rattle on cold start = WALK AWAY.

4a. Automatic Gearbox (if auto) Deal Breaker

Watch for lag, gear hunting, flare between gears or shudder under light acceleration. Tap N/A if it is a manual.

Verdict: Lower risk at 76k, but still WALK AWAY if it shudders.

4b. Clutch & Flywheel (if manual) Bargain Area

Pull away in 3rd at low revs. If the revs rise but the truck doesn't, the clutch is slipping. Listen for rattle at idle that stops when you press the clutch (dual mass flywheel). Tap N/A if it is an auto.

Bargaining Leverage: Clutch and flywheel is roughly £800 - £1,200 on these (estimate).

5. 4WD Super Select II Check Bargain Area

Stop, shift to N, turn the dial to 4H, roll forward slowly. Then try 4HLc and 4LLc. On a low-mileage truck these may rarely have been used, so make sure each one engages.

Bargaining Leverage: Flashing 4WD lights or clunks = demand £500 off.

6. Brakes & Handbrake Bargain Area

Check disc condition through the wheels. A big lip on the outer edge means they're worn. Test the handbrake on a slope. Brake firmly from 30mph: it should stop straight with no judder.

7. Tyres & Tracking Bargain Area

All four should be the same size and brand, with even wear and at least 3mm of tread. Check the date codes too: at 76k miles they could be old even with tread left. On a straight road, does it pull or wander?

Bargaining Leverage: About £100 per corner for decent tyres.

8. Barbarian Kit & Electrics Bargain Area

It's top trim, so test what you're paying for: sat nav and screen, reversing camera, heated leather seats, climate control and air con (cold air?), cruise control, electric windows and mirrors, all lights.

9. Paint & Bodywork Bargain Area

Black shows everything. Look along the panels in daylight for dents, scratches and mismatched paint. Check the load bed for heavy damage, and the bottoms of the doors and wheel arches for bubbling.

10. Leaks Underneath Bargain Area

Look under the engine, gearbox, transfer box and both diffs for fresh oil. Fresh underseal can hide old leaks, so look for wet patches on top of it. Check the coolant level and look for white crusty residue round the hoses.

Realistic Overall Discount Guide

The scripts don't stack. The dealer gives one overall figure.

Tips: Say your number, then stay quiet. Don't accept "trade sale" or "sold as seen" for a lower price. A dealer sale keeps your Consumer Rights Act protection.

The Age & Euro 5 Point Main Lever

Use early. Low miles are their strongest point, so this sets it against the age.

Worth: £250 - £750. Strongest if the Clean Air Zone checker shows it gets charged.
"The miles are good, but it's still an eleven-year-old truck, and it's only Euro 5. That means it gets charged in the Clean Air Zones, which cuts down who'll buy it and costs me money if I drive into Birmingham. The price needs to reflect that."

The Fresh Underseal Question

Use if they can't explain the underseal clearly.

Worth: £250 - £750. Drops to almost nothing if they show photos or an invoice proving the chassis was clean.
"Eight MOTs with nothing on it, then it gets undersealed right before it goes up for sale. That's fine if the chassis was clean, but nobody can see under it now, including me. If you can't show me what it looked like before, I'm taking that risk, and I need the price to cover it."

Service History at 76k

Use if the book is thin or missing.

Worth: £300 - £500. Or ask: "Do a full service before I collect."
"Low miles are only worth paying for if it's been looked after. Without a stamped book or invoices, I can't tell if it's had an oil change in years. I need that reflected in the price, or a full service done before I collect."

The Other Truck

Use when they say it's already a fair price.

Worth: £250 - £500. True: DX18 NVA is an 18-plate Warrior, Euro 6, up for £12,000.
"I'm also looking at an eighteen-plate Warrior at twelve grand. It's three years newer and Euro 6, so no Clean Air Zone charges. This one's got lower miles, which I like, but for the age it needs to come in under that one."

The Slow Market (Tax Change)

Use before your opening offer, to set the tone.

Worth: £250 - £500 on top of your other points (estimate). Pickup registrations are down 57% since HMRC started taxing double cabs as company cars in April 2025, so dealers have fewer business buyers.
"I've been keeping an eye on the market, and pickup sales have dropped through the floor since the tax change last year. Businesses just aren't buying them like they used to. I'm a private buyer and I'm ready to do a deal today, so let's find a number that works for both of us."
